A project management app for freelance interior designers juggling multiple projects should keep clients, budgets, suppliers and payments in one place, without the weight of an enterprise tool. Veta covers this with two plans built for independent designers: **Base**, free with 1 active project, and **Pro**, €25/month with 5 active projects, margin tracking, purchase orders and payments.

## What does a freelance interior designer need from a project management tool?

If you work solo or in a 1–3 person studio, your reality is specific: two or three clients at a time, no admin team, and a tight software budget. You do not need a hundred features. You need five that work well:

1. **Clients and suppliers** in tidy records, not scattered across email and your phone.
2. **Professional budgets** you can send without redesigning them every time.
3. **Cost tracking** that tells you whether a job is profitable before it closes, not after.
4. **A product catalog** you can reuse from one project to the next.
5. **An easy way to share progress** with your client.

Everything else (granular roles, corporate integrations, approval workflows) is cost and learning curve a freelancer rarely recovers.

## What goes wrong when you manage clients with generic tools?

Most independent designers start with spreadsheets, cloud folders and messaging apps. It works with one client. With three, things start to leak:

- **One spreadsheet per client:** no shared catalog, so you copy and paste the same products again and again.
- **Profitability in the dark:** costs and revenue live in separate sheets, and you only find out at the end.
- **Budgets that look improvised:** dated templates undermine your credibility.
- **Scattered updates:** the client gets ten PDFs and screenshots by email or chat.
- **Unrecorded change orders:** a verbal "yes" that never reaches the budget is lost money.
- **Forgotten suppliers:** you search for the same manufacturer on every project.

If three or more sound familiar, the problem is the tool, not you. We break it down in [Veta vs. spreadsheets for interior design budgets](/en/blog/veta-vs-spreadsheets-interior-design-budgets).

## Which features matter in day-to-day work?

### Budgets organized by room

In Veta, products are grouped by space (living room, kitchen, bedroom). The PDF budget keeps that structure, so the client understands what goes where and you do not maintain a separate sheet per room.

### Cost tracking without formulas

Each product stores its supplier cost and sale price. The app calculates totals (and, on Pro, margins) for you. No more hoping a spreadsheet formula survived the last inserted row.

### A professional PDF from day one

Budgets are generated with a polished layout, ready to send. You stop losing an afternoon formatting every new version.

## How do I reuse products across clients?

With the **product catalog**. Each product is saved once with its supplier, cost and reference price, and you add it to any project in a couple of clicks.

Over time, the catalog becomes an asset for a freelancer: your curated selection of furniture, lighting and finishes, tied to suppliers you already trust. Base gives you up to 50 products to start; Pro removes the limit.

## How do I share progress without sending ten PDFs?

With the **public project view**: a unique, read-only link you send to your client. They do not need an account or password, and they can see spaces, approved products, documents and recorded payments.

The key point: costs you mark as internal and your margins never show up. You decide what is shared. It is included on every plan, Base too. For more detail, read [why your client needs a public project view](/en/blog/client-public-view-interior-design).

## When should a freelancer move up to a studio plan?

The natural progression usually looks like this:

1. **Base** while you test the tool or have a single commission.
2. **Pro** as soon as you run two or more projects at once or need purchase orders and payments.
3. **[Studio](/en/studio-plan-for-architecture-and-interior-design-firms)** once you operate as a studio: more than 5 simultaneous projects, white-label budgets, currency and tax per project, or priority support.

Our take: do not upgrade "just in case." A freelancer with three or four clients in parallel is well served by Pro for a long time. Scale when the workload demands it, not before.
