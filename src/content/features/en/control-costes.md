Cost control and profitability in interior design answer two questions in one panel: **am I making money on this project?** and **does the budget match actual spend?** Profitability is revenue minus costs; margin expresses that result as a percentage. Reconciling estimated vs actual and payments vs purchase orders prevents discovering a loss at handover.

In Veta both readings come from the same cost-control module: estimated and actual totals, variance per line, product margin and — on Pro — payment coverage on purchase orders.

## Am I making money on this project?

Profitability is what remains after costs attributable to the job. Track three figures:

| Metric       | Formula                                | What it answers                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Revenue      | Fees + sales + approved extras         | What will you earn?             |
| Gross profit | Revenue − direct costs                 | What remains before overhead?   |
| Gross margin | Gross profit / revenue × 100           | How much of each euro remains?  |

**margin = (revenue − costs) / revenue × 100**

### Illustrative example: healthy margin

- Agreed revenue: €15,000
- Direct costs: €10,500
- Gross profit: €4,500
- Gross margin: **30%**

### Product margin

Sell a table for €1,200 with €800 cost: profit €400, margin **33.3%**. Veta totals product cost and sale price without spreadsheet formulas.

### Mistakes that shrink margin

1. Watching sold amount without committed cost.
2. Mixing client price and supplier cost without a clear rule.
3. Waiting until the end to log actual spend.
4. Not separating products, fees, construction and external services.
5. Absorbing extras without recording impact.

## Budget versus actual spend

Reconciliation means comparing the financial plan with reality **while** the project runs:

1. **Costs: estimated vs actual** — am I overrunning?
2. **Payments: budgeted vs paid** — am I financing the client's job?

### Variance per line

**variance = (actual − estimated) / estimated × 100**

| Variance          | Reading              |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Above +5%         | Overrun to review    |
| Between −5% and +5% | On plan            |
| Below −5%         | Saving               |
| No actual yet     | Pending              |

Illustrative example: carpentry estimated €3,000, invoice €3,600 → **+20%**. You know where to look before close.

### Purchase orders and payments (Pro plan)

Each payment can link to a purchase order. Veta marks coverage **covered**, **partial** or **pending**, so you know whether the client funded the buy before you confirm with the supplier.

### Unbudgeted costs

Add a new line with the actual amount. It increases total actual even without an estimate. If you pass it to the client, record the payment as an additional cost.

## Internal costs vs client view

The client budget shows sale prices, not your margin. Internal-cost lines stay out of the public view and PDF on compatible plans.

## FAQ

### How do I know if a project is profitable before close?

Update revenue and costs weekly and calculate margin with current figures, not only the original budget.

### What is gross margin?

Revenue minus direct costs as a percentage of revenue.

### What does reconciling budget and payments mean?

Crossing estimated vs actual per line and payments vs purchase orders while the job is still open.

### Does Veta read invoices or the bank?

No. You enter amounts; Veta calculates totals, variance and coverage.

### Can I hide internal costs?

Yes — flag lines as internal so they stay out of client views and documents.

### Which variance should I review first?

Those over 5%, high amounts, or pending purchases you cannot pass on to the client.
