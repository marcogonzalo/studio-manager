## What should a professional interior design budget PDF include?

A budget is not just a price list: it is the document your client uses to decide whether to trust you. A good interior design proposal answers three questions at a glance: what you will do, what they are buying, and what it all costs.

The essentials:

1. **Project header:** project name, client, date and studio details.
2. **Services:** your fees by phase (design, site supervision, procurement).
3. **Products by room:** furniture, lighting and finishes for each space, with quantity, unit price and line total.
4. **Tax:** VAT or sales tax, visible and correctly calculated.
5. **Totals:** subtotal, tax and grand total.
6. **Notes:** payment schedule, timeline, offer validity and exclusions.

## Why is Excel a poor budget template for client PDFs?

Spreadsheets are great at arithmetic, not at maintaining a document that changes every week. The cracks show the moment your client asks for revision two:

- **Every change is manual:** edit the cell, check the formula survived, fix page breaks, export again.
- **Version drift:** `budget_v3_FINAL_final.pdf` versus the live sheet, which is already on v5.
- **Leaking what should stay private:** forget to hide one column and the client sees your trade price.
- **Fragile layout:** adding one product to a room pushes the rest of the document out of place.

It is not a discipline problem; it is a tooling problem. We compare both approaches in [Veta vs. spreadsheets for interior design budgets](/en/blog/veta-vs-spreadsheets-interior-design-budgets).

## How should you structure the budget: services, products by room and tax?

Clients read budgets best when **what you do** (services) is separated from **what they buy** (products), and each block is grouped logically:

| Block    | How it is grouped       | Example                                |
| -------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Services | By phase and category   | Design phase → concept design          |
| Products | By room or space        | Living room → sofa, coffee table, lamp |
| Tax      | Single rate or per rate | VAT 21% on the subtotal                |
| Totals   | Subtotal + tax          | Grand total including tax              |
| Notes    | Free text at the end    | Payments, timeline, exclusions         |

Grouping products by room has a practical upside: clients mentally walk through their home as they read, so questions become specific ("is the living room lamp the floor one?") instead of a vague "this feels expensive".

## Interior design budget example

Illustrative example, not a real client budget. Amounts in euros with 21% VAT; swap in your own currency and tax rate.

**Header:** Apartment refurbishment, 12 X Street · Client: Mary Green · January 15, 2026 · Your interior design studio.

**Services (budget lines)**

| Phase  | Line item              | Amount |
| ------ | ---------------------- | ------ |
| Design | Concept design         | €2,000 |
| Design | Detailed design        | €3,500 |
| Build  | Site supervision       | €4,000 |
| Build  | Procurement management | €1,200 |

**Products by room**

| Room         | Product         | Price × qty | Total  |
| ------------ | --------------- | ----------- | ------ |
| Living room  | Sofa, model X   | €1,800 × 1  | €1,800 |
| Living room  | Coffee table    | €600 × 1    | €600   |
| Living room  | Floor lamp      | €350 × 2    | €700   |
| Main bedroom | Upholstered bed | €1,200 × 1  | €1,200 |
| Main bedroom | Bedside tables  | €280 × 2    | €560   |

**Totals:** services €10,700 + products €4,860 = subtotal €15,560. VAT 21%: €3,267.60. **Total: €18,827.60.**

**Notes:** 40% on signing, 30% at mid-build, 30% on completion. Timeline: 12 weeks from confirmation.

## How do I keep internal costs out of the client PDF?

The PDF is built for the client, so it **never shows a product's supplier cost or your margin**, only the sale price. That holds on every plan.

On the [Pro](/en/pro-plan-for-independent-interior-designers) and Studio plans you get more control:

- **Internal cost lines:** flag a line as an internal cost (say, delivery you absorb) and it stays off the client budget while still counting in your cost control.
- **Exclude products without deleting them:** a rejected product leaves the budget but stays in the project in case the client changes their mind.
- **Multi-rate tax:** each product and line can carry its own tax rate, and the PDF breaks the total down by rate.

## How do I update the budget without rebuilding the PDF?

Change the data at the source and regenerate. If the sofa goes up in price, edit the product; if the client adds a lamp, add it to the room. On the next export, subtotals, tax and grand total recalculate and the layout holds.

In practice, the PDF stops being a document you maintain and becomes a view of your project. What you send always matches what you are working with.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my client see my costs or margin in the PDF?

No. The PDF shows sale prices, never supplier costs or margin. On Pro and Studio you can also flag lines as internal costs so they stay hidden.

### Does the PDF show tax per line or only on the total?

With a single rate, one percentage applies to everything. With multi-rate tax (Pro and Studio), each line carries its own rate and the PDF shows the breakdown by rate.

### Can I add payment terms and timelines?

Yes. Budget notes are printed at the end of the PDF on every plan.
